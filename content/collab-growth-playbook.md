# Collab Growth Playbook — Epilepsy Collab & Brain Tumor Collab

Draft v0.1 · 2026-09-25 · companion to
[`../docs/content-creation-engine.md`](../docs/content-creation-engine.md)

Two regional, cross-institution, peer-to-peer networks. Each is a website plus recurring
virtual meetings and in-person regional meetings. They exist so the clinicians who share a
patient also know each other, in each catchment area.

> **The network is the product. Content is the front door. Meetings are where the value
> happens.** Every post, ad, and email should move someone one step along:
> *see → follow → sign up → attend → present a case → bring a colleague.*

---

## 1. Shared foundations

### Why regional, and why peer-to-peer
National societies (AES, ILAE, SNO, AANS/CNS) set standards and hold one big annual meeting.
Hospital systems run tumor boards and epilepsy conferences inside their own walls. What is
usually missing is the **cross-institution, same-region relationship**: the epileptologist at
the community practice who has never met the surgeon 40 minutes away, or the rad onc and med
onc who share a glioma patient but only meet through the chart. The Collabs fill that gap.
§8 covers what else already exists and where the Collabs differ from it.

### Governance decisions to make first
These shape everything downstream, including what the ads can say:

1. **Who convenes it.** Physician co-chairs per region (one surgeon + one epileptologist; one
   neurosurgeon + one neuro-onc/rad onc) give it credibility and keep it peer-to-peer.
2. **Who funds it.** Independent (physician-led, society-style) vs. industry-supported. If
   industry money or an industry employee is involved, that shapes meal rules, reporting, and
   disclosure (see §7). Decide before the first in-person meeting.
3. **CME or not.** Accredited CME raises attendance and legitimacy, but it requires an
   accredited provider (often a hospital or university) and independence from commercial
   interests in content.
4. **Case privacy.** Cases discussed across institutions are de-identified by default.

### The flywheel
```
Clinical post → follow the Collab page → join the list (website)
      ↑                                        ↓
Meeting recap post  ←  present a case  ←  attend a virtual/in-person meeting
      ↓
"Bring a colleague" → new member in the same region
```
Measure each arrow (§6).

## 2. Epilepsy Collab

### Problem, in one breath
Epilepsy surgery is one of the most effective and least used treatments in neurology. Many
patients with drug-resistant epilepsy wait years for a surgical evaluation, and a large share
are never referred. The delay is often not about surgical skill. It is about the relationship
and the handoff between the people managing seizures and the people who can operate.
*(Citable numbers: §2a.)*

_Filled from the landscape research — see bottom of section._

### Audience
| Tier | Who | What they get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Epileptologists (adult & pediatric), epilepsy/functional neurosurgeons | A standing regional case conference; a named surgeon or epileptologist to call |
| Extended (phase 2) | General and community neurologists who manage epilepsy | A low-friction path to "is this patient a surgical candidate?" — this is where most referral delay lives |
| Supporting | Neuropsychologists, neuroradiologists, EEG/SEEG teams, epilepsy nurses, APPs | A seat at the case discussion |

### Positioning statement
For epilepsy surgeons and epileptologists who share patients but rarely share a room,
**Epilepsy Collab** is the regional network that puts them in the same conversation, online
every month and in person each quarter, so patients with drug-resistant epilepsy reach a
surgical discussion sooner. Unlike national meetings or single-hospital conferences, it is
local, cross-institution, and built around real (de-identified) cases.

### Tagline options
- *Shorten the road to surgery.*
- *Know your surgeon. Know your epileptologist.*
- *Two failed medications should start a conversation. We host it.*
- *Your region's epilepsy surgery conference.*

### Meeting formats
- **Monthly virtual case conference** (45–60 min, evening): 2–3 de-identified cases, one
  presented by a community epileptologist, one by a surgical center. Standing agenda item:
  "one paper that should change what we do" (drawn from the content engine).
- **Quarterly in-person regional meeting**: a short talk, case discussion, and time to meet.
  The in-person meeting builds the phone-call relationships.
- **Annual regional summit** (once there are 2+ active regions).

### Content emphasis
The Referral Gap, Hypothetical Case ("Would you refer? When?"), The Breakdown, Practice Poll,
Community (meeting announcements and recaps). Awareness anchors: International Epilepsy Day
(February), Purple Day (March 26), Epilepsy Awareness Month (November), AES Annual Meeting
(December).

## 3. Brain Tumor Collab

### Problem, in one breath
A brain-tumor patient's care passes through five to eight specialties in the first months:
surgery, pathology and molecular testing, radiation, systemic therapy, imaging, and
rehabilitation or palliative care. Outcomes depend on the handoffs. Multidisciplinary review
is standard at large centers but uneven across a region, and the clinicians involved often
work at different institutions. *(Citable numbers: §3a.)*

### §3a. Numbers for copy (verified, cited)
_Filled from the landscape research — see bottom of section._

### Audience
| Tier | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Core | Neurosurgeons, neuro-oncologists, radiation oncologists, medical oncologists |
| Extended | Neuroradiologists, neuropathologists, APPs, nurse navigators, rehab, palliative care, social work |
| Adjacent | Community oncologists who see brain metastases first |

### Positioning statement
For the surgeons, oncologists, and radiation oncologists who share brain-tumor patients across
a region, **Brain Tumor Collab** is the network that turns a chain of referrals into a team,
through a regional virtual tumor conference, in-person meetings, and a shared read on new
evidence. Unlike a single hospital's tumor board, it spans institutions, so the patient's
whole care team can be in the same room.

### Tagline options
- *Every brain tumor patient has a team. Let's meet it.*
- *The handoffs are the treatment plan.*
- *One region. One tumor board.*
- *Surgery, radiation, systemic therapy — one conversation.*

### Meeting formats
- **Monthly virtual regional tumor conference**: 2–3 de-identified cases, with at least three
  specialties presenting on each. Rotate the lead specialty.
- **Quarterly in-person meeting**: themed (brain metastases, glioma, meningioma, radiation
  necrosis / treatment effect), with a short talk from each specialty's point of view.
- **"Handoff" sessions**: 20-minute joint talks by two specialties on one transition point
  (e.g., post-op imaging to radiation start; SRS to systemic therapy for brain mets).

### Content emphasis
The Handoff, The Breakdown, Numbers That Matter, Hypothetical Case, Conference Dispatch
(ASTRO, SNO, ASCO), Community. Awareness anchors: Brain Tumor Awareness Month (May), World
Brain Tumour Day (June 8), Glioblastoma Awareness Day (third Wednesday of July), SNO Annual
Meeting (November).

## 4. Launch sequence (same for both Collabs, per region)

| Phase | Timing | Actions | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0. Founding members** | Weeks −8 to −4 | Recruit 2 physician co-chairs, then 10–15 founding members through personal outreach. Book the first virtual meeting and one in-person date. | None public. Author's personal LinkedIn runs clinical posts to build the audience. |
| **1. Soft launch** | Weeks −4 to 0 | Website live with a sign-up form; LinkedIn page created; founding members follow and share. | 3–4 clinical posts on the Collab page so it isn't empty at launch. |
| **2. Public launch** | Week 0 | Launch post from the Collab page, co-chair posts, and the author's post; first meeting invitation. | "Why we started this" + the core statistic + invitation. |
| **3. First meeting** | Weeks 2–4 | Run it well; keep it to time; collect 2 cases for next month before people leave. | Save-the-date → reminder → recap post with a takeaway (no patient details). |
| **4. Steady state** | Monthly | Meeting rhythm + 2 clinical posts/week + 1 community post/week. Start paid promotion once the page has proof (recap posts, member count). | Content engine batches. |
| **5. Expand** | Quarter 2+ | Second region; member-get-member invitations; conference presence. | Region launch posts; member spotlights. |

## 5. Promotion kit

### Meeting announcement (LinkedIn, Collab page)
> **[Region] Epilepsy Collab — [Month] Case Conference**
> [Day, Date] · [Time] [TZ] · Virtual (45 min)
>
> Two de-identified cases, one question each: *Is this patient a surgical candidate, and
> what would you do next?* Presented by [Name, role] and [Name, role].
>
> Open to epileptologists, epilepsy surgeons, and neurologists in [region].
> Register: [link in comments]
>
> Have a case you'd like the group's input on? Reply or message us.

### Reminder (day before)
> Tomorrow at [time]: [Region] Epilepsy Collab case conference. Bring a colleague; the
> conversation is better with more of the region in it. [link in comments]

### Recap (within 48 hours)
> At last night's [Region] Brain Tumor Collab conference, [n] clinicians from [n]
> institutions discussed [topic]. The takeaway that got the most debate:
> **[one-sentence clinical takeaway, no patient details].**
> Where does your practice land on this? Next meeting: [date].

### Call for cases
> We're building next month's agenda. The most useful cases are the ones where reasonable
> people disagree: timing, candidacy, sequencing. Send us a de-identified summary: [link].

### Email invitation to a founding member (from a co-chair)
> Subject: Would you help start a regional [epilepsy surgery / brain tumor] conference?
>
> Dr. [Name] — a few of us in [region] want to meet regularly across institutions: one
> evening a month online to talk through cases, and in person once a quarter. No sponsor
> pitch, no presentation to prepare unless you want to. I'd value having you as a founding
> member. Could we have 15 minutes this week?

## 6. Advertising & growth

### Where to spend (in order)
1. **Organic first.** Founding members, co-chairs, and the author's personal network. Paid
   ads amplify proof; they don't create it. Start paid only after 2–3 meetings and a few recap
   posts.
2. **LinkedIn Sponsored Content** (single image or document ad) and **Event Ads** for
   meetings, targeted by region, job title, specialty and skills, and a matched list of
   invited physicians. Retarget website visitors via the LinkedIn Insight Tag.
3. **Society channels**: regional neurological/neurosurgical society newsletters, state
   epilepsy foundation affiliates, conference satellite meetups (AES, SNO).
4. **Referral loop**: every meeting ends with "who in our region should be here?" and a
   one-click invitation email.

§6a._

Targets are starting points to revise after the first quarter, not benchmarks.
