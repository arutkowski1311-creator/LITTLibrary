### li-01 · Two Studies, One Question · target Thu Oct 1
**Sources:** `intrago-ii-iort-newly-gbm-lancetoncol-2026`, `ultrahypofx-6x6gy-vs-30x2gy-gbm-radiotheroncol-2026`

**Hook A:** More radiation in the cavity didn't help. Fewer radiation visits hurt. Two phase 3 glioblastoma trials this summer, and one uncomfortable question.

**Hook B:** This summer, two phase 3 trials tried to improve on standard radiation for newly diagnosed glioblastoma from opposite directions. Neither did.

**Post**

> More radiation in the cavity didn't help. Fewer radiation visits hurt. Two phase 3 glioblastoma trials this summer, and one uncomfortable question.
>
> 1) INTRAGO-II (Lancet Oncology, July 2026). 314 patients were randomized to a 30 Gy intraoperative radiotherapy boost at resection or to standard care. Median PFS was 11.0 vs 11.4 months (HR 1.1, p=0.47). Local recurrence dominated in both arms (72% vs 71%). The boost added toxicity: grade 3–4 seizures 13% vs 7%, radiation necrosis 7% vs 2%.
>
> 2) A Dutch multicenter phase 3 (Radiotherapy & Oncology, August 2026) compared 6 × 6 Gy with standard 30 × 2 Gy chemoradiation. Median OS was 13.0 vs 21.0 months. The curves overlapped for about 6 months, then separated (HR 2.54 after that point). Necrosis or pseudoprogression occurred in 47.8% vs 16.2%.
>
> The catch: the Dutch trial stopped at 135 of 474 planned patients, so its estimates are imprecise. INTRAGO-II was open-label, with 17 months' median follow-up.
>
> Taken together, 60 Gy in 30 fractions is proving hard to beat by changing dose or schedule. And ~70% local failure even after a 30 Gy cavity boost hints that the target sits beyond the cavity. That last part is a hypothesis, not a finding.
>
> If more cavity dose doesn't improve local control, where would you bet on the next real gain in newly diagnosed GBM: resection beyond the enhancing margin, better imaging for targeting, or systemic and immune therapy?
>
> Giordano FA et al. Lancet Oncol 2026;27(7):864-878. doi:10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00235-4
> de Jong et al. Radiother Oncol 2026;223:111726. doi:10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111726
>
> Views my own. I work in the neurosurgical device industry.

**First comment:** Links: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(26)00235-4 · https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111726 — One more detail from INTRAGO-II: serious adverse events occurred in 65% of IORT patients vs 53% with standard care.

### li-03 · Myth vs. Data · target Thu Oct 8
**Sources:** `eortc-22033-mature-lgg-tmz-vs-rt-jco-2026` (with its PROGRES companion paper)

**Hook A:** For two decades, "over 40" has helped decide who gets treated right after surgery for a low-grade glioma. New JCO data say that rule doesn't hold for IDH-mutant tumors.

**Hook B:** Myth: in low-grade glioma, age over 40 means high risk. Data from summer 2026: not in IDH-mutant tumors.

**Post**

> For two decades, "over 40" has helped decide who gets treated right after surgery for a low-grade glioma. New JCO data say that rule doesn't hold for IDH-mutant tumors.
>
> Two Journal of Clinical Oncology papers this summer:
>
> 1) Mature EORTC 22033 (478 randomized; 351 reclassified under WHO 2021). First-line temozolomide and radiotherapy alone gave no difference in PFS or OS. Median OS for IDH-mutant astrocytoma was about 6.6–6.7 years in either arm. Patients 40 and older did better than those under 40.
>
> 2) PROGRES pooled 1,619 patients from 11 prospective trials and registries, then validated in 1,292 more. Age was associated with PFS in IDH-wildtype glioma (HR 1.74), but not in IDH-mutant glioma (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.76–1.05). Nor did age predict who benefited from chemoradiation in IDH-mutant tumors.
>
> The catch: EORTC 22033 tested single treatments, not the combined therapy that is now standard for many patients. The molecular reclassification was post hoc, and PROGRES pools older, heterogeneous cohorts.
>
> My read (promising, not settled): the post-op decision is moving from the birth date to the pathology report and the residual tumor on MRI.
>
> If age drops out, what should drive the choice between surveillance, an IDH inhibitor, and chemoradiation after surgery: residual volume, CDKN2A/B status, growth rate, or something else?
>
> Baumert BG et al. J Clin Oncol 2026. doi:10.1200/JCO-25-02735
> Kinslow CJ et al. J Clin Oncol 2026. doi:10.1200/JCO-25-01846
>
> Views my own. I work in the neurosurgical device industry.

**First comment:** Links: https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-02735 · https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-01846 — In the same EORTC 22033 analysis, oligodendroglioma OS was 12.9 years with RT vs 14.9 years with TMZ (HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.52–1.49).

**Post**

>

**Source:** `eano-radiation-necrosis-consensus-neurooncol-2026`

**Hook A:** Radiation necrosis is the classic ownership gap. Rad onc caused it, neuroradiology names it, neuro-onc treats it, and the surgeon gets the call when that fails.

**Hook B:** EANO has published its first consensus statement on radiation necrosis. 53 of 57 statements reached agreement.

**Post**

> Radiation necrosis is the classic ownership gap. Rad onc caused it, neuroradiology names it, neuro-onc treats it, and the surgeon gets the call when that fails.
>
> A new EANO consensus (Neuro-Oncology, July 2026; 20 experts, 3-round Delphi, 53 of 57 statements agreed) puts a name on each step:
> • Incidence 4–30%, typically 6–24 months after radiotherapy
> • Perfusion MRI and amino-acid PET are the most suitable tools for telling necrosis from recurrence. Histology remains the gold standard but is hard to read after radiation.
> • Steroids may be tried first. Bevacizumab for steroid-refractory necrosis.
> • Surgery for rapid symptom relief and diagnosis in accessible lesions. Laser ablation can be considered as an additional option for symptomatic necrosis.
>
> The catch: this is expert opinion from a European panel, not trial evidence, and the authors call for randomized trials in symptomatic necrosis.
>
> What it gives a region: a shared way to agree who triggers the next step.
>
> For a steroid-dependent patient with a single accessible necrotic focus, is bevacizumab first the right default? Or do tissue confirmation and faster steroid withdrawal justify going local first?
>
> Duerinck J et al. Neuro Oncol 2026. doi:10.1093/neuonc/noag153
